Command Bridge
How it works Writing GitHub

Talk to your coding agents, and see what they mean.

Command Bridge gives your agents a voice and a canvas. You speak; they answer out loud and draw the answer while they explain it. It runs on your own machine, with no account and no speech API.

Why I built it

I kept hitting the same wall with my agents: getting what I meant in was slow, and getting what they meant out was slower.

1

Typing to an agent

I type a request, read a wall of text back, and type again. Most of my day with AI agents went into that loop.

2

Talking is three times faster

Dictation let me explain my reasoning instead of abbreviating it, and the results got closer to what I imagined.

3

Reading is the bottleneck

Reading the answer back is the slow half. What if I could hear it, and see what it means? Intent and understanding per minute

4

So I built Command Bridge

Each agent keeps working in its own terminal. Command Bridge carries my voice to it, and its voice and drawings back to my laptop or my phone. The agent points at the part it is talking about. The agent points

One voice, a whole team

I talk to one agent, my chief of staff. The others work under it and brief it, so a dozen agents never talk over each other. I let my AI agent manage my attention

A voice turn is measured end to end, so I can see where the waiting goes. From 15 seconds to under 1

Local

Speech recognition, the voice and the voiceprint run on your machine. No account and no speech API. A CPU is enough, and a GPU makes it faster.

Any agent

The tool holds no model. If your agent can run a command, it can talk to you. Claude Code, Codex and Grok have each driven it unchanged.

One command to learn it

command-bridge describe hands your agent the whole contract, so it sets itself up and knows how to listen.
